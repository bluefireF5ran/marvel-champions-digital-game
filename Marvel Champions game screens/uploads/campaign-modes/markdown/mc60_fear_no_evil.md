## Page 3: FEATURED KEYWORDS
*Source: mc60_rulebook-web.pdf (Page 3 of 28)*

#### **_FEATURED KEYWORDS_** 

- Keyword new to this expansion. 

- ** Keyword updated starting with this expansion. 

###### **Discount X (** trait **)** * 

When a player plays a card with discount X, its resource cost is reduced by X if that player’s identity has the specified trait. If more than one trait is specified and the player’s identity has at least one of the specified traits, the cost is reduced by X. 

###### **Hinder X** 

When a card with hinder X enters play, place X additional threat on that card. 

###### **Patrol** 

While a minion with patrol is engaged with a player, that player cannot use cards they control to thwart the main scheme. 

###### **Permanent** 

A card with permanent cannot be defeated, leave play, or have any part of its text box blanked, except by card abilities in the same set (hero set, scenario set, or modular set). 

###### **Piercing** 

An attack with piercing discards any tough status cards from the target of the attack before dealing damage. 

###### **Prerequisite (form or** trait **) *** 

In order for a player to play a card with prerequisite (form) or prerequisite (trait), their identity must be in the specified form (i.e. Hero or Alter-Ego form) or have the specified trait. 

###### **Ranged** 

An attack with ranged ignores the retaliate keyword. 

###### **Setup** 

A card with setup begins the game in play. 

###### **Stalwart** 

A character with stalwart cannot be stunned or confused. 

###### **Starting** * 

A card with starting may be added to its owner’s starting hand during setup. Each deck cannot include more than one card with starting. Before drawing their starting hand during player setup, each player with a starting card in their deck may search their deck for that card and add it as the first card of their starting hand. That card does count against their hand size and can be discarded for the mulligan. 

###### **Steady** 

A character with steady can have one additional stunned status card and one additional confused status card. That character is not stunned unless they have two stunned status cards, and is not confused unless they have two confused status cards. After that character’s attack, scheme, or thwart is canceled by a status card effect, remove all status cards of the corresponding type from that character. 

###### **Team-Up** 

The team-up keyword names two characters. To include a card with team-up in a player’s deck, their chosen identity must match one of the named characters. Additionally, a card with team-up cannot be played unless both of the named characters (identity or ally) are in play. 

###### **Teamwork (** trait **)** 

After a minion with teamwork enters play and engages a player, if there is at least one other minion that shares the specified trait in play, the minion that just entered play activates against the player it is engaged with. 

**_Example_** : Kate is engaged with Ivan Banionis when Johnny reveals and engages a Tracksuit Bro, who has Teamwork (tracksuit). Because Ivan Banionis also has the tracksuit trait, the Tracksuit Bro activates against Johnny. 

###### **Temporary** 

A card with temporary must be discarded from play at the end of the round. 

###### **Victory X** 

When a card with victory X is defeated, place it in the victory display instead of its owner’s discard pile. 

###### **Villainous** ** 

When a character with villainous uses a basic power, give it a facedown boost card from the top of the encounter deck. When resolving that basic power, turn the boost card faceup, resolve any boost ability on that card, and apply its boost icons to the character’s stats for this use. Discard the boost card after the basic power resolves. 

###### **Vulnerable** ** 

When a character with vulnerable becomes stunned or confused, that character is immediately discarded. That character is not considered defeated. If the character would be simultaneously defeated by damage and become stunned or confused, the first player chooses whether that character is considered defeated or not.

## Page 4: FEATURED TERMS
*Source: mc60_rulebook-web.pdf (Page 4 of 28)*

#### **_FEATURED TERMS_** 

#### **_PLAYER SIDE SCHEMES_** 

- Term new to this expansion. 

Player side schemes are a type of player card. During a player’s turn, they can play a player side scheme from their hand by paying its resource cost, placing it next to the main scheme, and putting threat on it equal to its starting threat value. While in play, player side schemes function the same as encounter side schemes, except that they are player cards. 

- ** Term updated starting with this expansion. 

###### **“Exactly Defeat”** * 

When a card ability requires a character to “exactly defeat” an enemy, that enemy must be defeated by an attack that dealt no excess damage. 

When a card ability requires a character to “exactly defeat” a side scheme, that side scheme must be defeated by a thwart that did not attempt to remove more threat than was necessary to defeat the scheme. For example, a thwart that can remove two threat from a scheme can exactly defeat a scheme with two threat on it, but not a scheme with one threat on it. 

Player side schemes are subject to the **player side scheme limit** , which restricts the players to having at most one player side scheme in play if one or two players started the game, or at most two player side schemes if three or four players started the game. If this limit is exceeded, the first player chooses and discards player side schemes from play to their owners’ discard piles until they no longer exceed the limit. 

###### **“Find”** ** 

When an effect instructs a player to find a card, they search each area where the card could be found (play area, set-aside area, player deck, discard pile, encounter deck, etc.). They do **not** search the victory display, cards that have been removed from the game, facedown encounter cards currently dealt to players, or facedown boost cards. Do not search game areas unnecessarily if it is known where the card can be found. 

#### **_ATTACKS AGAINST ALLIES_** 

Some effects cause a villain or minion to attack an ally directly. When this occurs, any undefended damage from that attack is placed on the ally that was attacked. 

Any boost abilities or triggered abilities that resolve during an attack against an ally that refer to “you” refer to the player who controls the attacked ally. Player card abilities that trigger when the attacking enemy “attacks you” do not trigger. 

###### **“Tuck”** 

When a player is instructed to tuck a card under another card, they place the tucked card faceup under the other card. The tucked card is not in play. When a card leaves play, any cards tucked under it are discarded. 

Players may defend these attacks as normal by declaring a hero or an ally as the defender. If the attack has overkill and defeats an ally (whether that ally was the attacked ally or a defending ally), any excess damage from that attack is dealt to the identity of the player who controlled the defeated ally. 

#### **_VICTORY DISPLAY_** 

The victory display is an out-of-play game area shared by all players. Cards in the victory display follow the standard rules for out-of-play cards. 

#### **_ALTERNATE ENCOUNTER CARD BACK_** 

#### **_FORCED ACTIONS_** 

Some cards have a “ **Forced Action** ” triggered ability. A player may perform a forced action at any time during the player phase they could normally perform an action, but the player phase cannot end until all possible forced actions among all players have been performed. If a card with a forced action ability leaves play, or the cost cannot otherwise be paid (such as if the cost includes exhausting an exhausted card), then the action cannot be resolved and the players can end the player phase as normal. 

This product contains a variation on the standard encounter card back. This card back allows players to differentiate these cards from other cards of the same type as the rules for these cards prevent them from entering a deck, a discard pile, or a player’s hand. 

#### **_AMPLIFY ICON (_** [star] **_) (UPDATED)_** 

The amplify icon increases the number of boost icons on boost cards. When a character resolves a boost card while using a basic power, add one additional boost icon to that card for each amplify icon in play.

## **_INTERCHANGEABLE SCENARIOS_** 

Unlike other Marvel Champions expansions, five of this expansion’s scenarios combine one of five interchangeable main schemes with one of the five interchangeable villains with the underling trait. The setup instructions for each scenario instruct the players to choose an underling villain to play against. 

#### **_UNDERLING VILLAIN SETUP AND PLAY TIPS_** 

The following sections provide setup instructions and play tips for each underling villain. For a flavorful rundown of all of the villains, check out Jessica Jones’s notes on the next page. 

###### **BULLSEYE** 

To set up Bullseye, use Bullseye (I) and (II). (Use Bullseye (II) and (III) instead for expert mode.) 

Each of Bullseye’s boost cards gets an additional boost icon, which makes him particularly dangerous if he gains extra boost cards by Raise the Stakes or various “ **Boost** ” abilities on treachery cards. Many of Bullseye’s encounter cards target either your allies or your persona supports and can remove them from the game. Note that if this happens during a campaign, those allies and supports are gone for the rest of the campaign! With Bullseye (II), Bullseye’s permanent Adamantium-Laced Spine attachment prevents all but 3 damage from each attack, meaning he is more susceptible to small attacks. If you manage to flip the attachment, make the most of this opportunity with big attacks. 

###### **ELECTRO** 

To set up Electro, use Electro (I) and (II). (Use Electro (II) and (III) instead for expert mode.) 

Electro places charge counters on her permanent Electric Charge attachment, and she spends those counters to give herself additional boost cards. She replenishes these charges when she schemes and through various encounter card effects. Conversely, players can remove these counters by spending resources. Energy ([wild]) resources are double-edged swords as they are more effective at removing charges, but also make players more susceptible to her treacheries. 

###### **HAMMERHEAD** 

To set up Hammerhead, use Hammerhead (I) and (II). (Use Hammerhead (II) and (III) instead for expert mode.) 

Hammerhead is all about stunning his enemies. His “ **Forced Response** ” stuns any character that he damages. The “Head First!” treachery and Maggia Underboss minions hit even harder when your hero is stunned. Use tough status cards, your hero’s DEF value, and expendable allies to keep your hero from being stunned. 

###### **PURPLE MAN** 

To set up Purple Man, use Purple Man (I) and (II). (Use Purple Man (II) and (III) instead for expert mode.) 

Purple Man’s stats may be low, but that’s because he makes others do his dirty work. Each of his cards gains a “ **Boost** ” ability that deals that card as a facedown encounter card after the activation. Each card has a star (• ) in the boost field to remind you of this. Purple Man’s encounter set includes several Influenced minions—mind-controlled citizens who punish you if you defeat them. Purple Man gives these minions guard (and villainous and patrol at higher stages). However, since they all have the vulnerable keyword, they can be safely discarded if stunned or confused. 

###### **TYPHOID MARY** 

To set up Typhoid Mary, reveal the Establish Trust side scheme ( 113A) and the Disturbed Psyche environment ( 112). Resolve the “ **Setup** ” ability on Disturbed Psyche to put the Typhoid Mary (A)/Bloody Mary (A) card into play on a random side. (Use Typhoid Mary (B)/Bloody Mary (B) instead for Expert mode). 

The villain flips at the end of each villain phase, switching between her Typhoid Mary form and her Bloody Mary form. This means that her attack and thwart will alternate being her higher stat—so plan accordingly. The players do not win this scenario in the usual way (defeating all the villain stages), but instead by getting three tokens on the Disturbed Psyche environment. The players gain tokens either by removing all the threat from Establish Trust or by reducing the villain’s hit points to 0. Establish Trust adds threat tokens to Disturbed Psyche, while the villain’s “ **Forced Interrupt** ” adds damage tokens. In campaign play, if Disturbed Psyche has more threat than damage when the players win, they gain access to a Typhoid Mary/Bloody Mary ally for the rest of the campaign.

#### **_CAMPAIGN MODE RULES_** 

Each of the scenarios in this expansion can be played individually as a standalone adventure, or together with the other scenarios as part of one overarching campaign. Campaign mode combines five of the six scenarios in the Fear No Evil expansion into one epic experience, where the outcome of each game impacts the next scenario. 

Unlike other Marvel Champions campaigns, the order of these scenarios is not fixed. Instead, the players choose which scenario to play next after each game. 

To begin a campaign, the players start by choosing their heroes. Each player must use their chosen identity for the entire campaign, but they are free to change aspects and alter the contents of their deck between scenarios, following the deck customization rules in the Marvel Champions Rules Reference. 

###### **CAMPAIGN LOG** 

The campaign log found on page 28 of this rulebook tracks the progress of the entire campaign. At the end of each scenario, the players record their results by entering the required information in the campaign log. 

A printable copy of the campaign log can be found on our support page at: 

www.FantasyFlightGames.com/Marvel-Champions 

###### **SCENARIO PROGRESSION** 

Before choosing each scenario—including the first—two of the scenarios that haven’t been resolved yet are chosen at random. These scenarios progress (this is explained in detail in campaign setup) as the villain continues with their plot while the heroes are occupied elsewhere. If a scenario progresses too much, the players automatically fail that scenario and receive a penalty for the rest of the campaign. Losing a scenario does not automatically cause it to fail and it may be able to be attempted again. Conversely, winning a scenario earns the players a benefit for the rest of the campaign. 

Before playing each scenario in campaign mode, follow the instructions in the Campaign Setup section on page 9. When the game ends, if the players won, follow that scenario’s victory instructions in the order they are listed. If the players lost, follow that scenario’s defeat instructions instead. 

###### **CAMPAIGN ENVIRONMENTS** 

Each interchangeable main scheme has a corresponding environment card. These cards are double-sided, with a Completed side and a Failed side. The Completed side indicates what benefits the players gain by playing and winning that scenario. The Failed side indicates what penalties the players suffer if that scenario has failed. Players may look at both sides of each of these cards when choosing which scenario to play. 

Once a scenario is completed or failed, the players begin each subsequent game with that scenario’s environment in play on its appropriate side. 

###### **UNDERLING VILLAIN PLAY ORDER** 

As mentioned earlier, each scenario uses one of the five interchangeable villains. Each of these villains has the underling trait. For the players’ first playthrough, below is a recommended order to play against the villains. This order pits the heroes against increasingly complex villains as the campaign advances. 

**~~1~~** Hammerhead 

- **~~2~~** Bullseye 

- **~~3~~** Electro 

- **~~4~~** Purple Man or Typhoid Mary 

On subsequent campaign playthroughs, players should randomly select the underling villain for each game. 

Once all five interchangeable main schemes are either Completed or Failed, the players take on Kingpin in a final showdown!

#### **_EXPERT CAMPAIGN_** 

For players who want an even more challenging campaign experience, the Fear No Evil expansion comes with everything you need to play an expert campaign. 

- **»** Some Setup and Victory instructions are preceded by **Expert Campaign Only** . Ignore these instructions unless you are playing an expert campaign. 

###### **PERSISTENT DAMAGE** 

While playing the Fear No Evil campaign at the expert level, each player must record their remaining hit points in the campaign log after **each** game. This determines each player’s starting hit points for the next scenario. 

- **»** If a player’s remaining hit points is greater than their base hit points, record their base hit points in the campaign log instead. 

###### **ELIMINATION AND VICTORY** 

In an expert campaign, if a player is defeated during a scenario that their teammates go on to win, the defeated player does not participate in the Victory steps of that scenario. 

However, during the Setup instructions of the next scenario, the defeated player can rejoin their teammates for the next scenario by following the Campaign Setup instructions for healing their identity.

#### **_PROTECTION RACKET_** 

_*9-1-1. What is your emergency?*_ 

_*Some guys in my dad’s shop are threatening to hurt him if he doesn’t pay! Please, send help quick!*_ 

_You arrive at the small mom-and-pop shop to find several goons with baseball bats smashing the place up as its proprietor takes cover behind the counter._ 

_“I told you, old man, protection money’s due the first of the month,” the biggest goon says. “This is what we call a ‘late fee’.” The goon winds up his bat, and you catch it on the backswing and rip it from his grasp._ 

_“So, gramps called for some backup, eh?” the goon says, taking a few steps back. Just then, the bell above the front door tinkles as the door opens. You glance over your shoulder at the new threat entering the store. “Good thing I did, too.”_ 

**Main Scheme Deck** : Protection Racket (x5) 

**Encounter Deck** : Protection Racket, Disasters, Tracksuit Mafia, Standard, and chosen villain’s encounter sets. (The Standard set can be found in the Marvel Champions core set.) #### **_SEPARATE MAIN SCHEMES_**

###### **TYPHOID MARY** 

# **<u>DAREDEVIL’S</u> SENSE DECK** 

Daredevil’s superhuman senses are represented by his Sense deck. To create the sense deck, shuffle all five of Daredevil’s Sense cards together. Place it faceup next to your identity and you can look through it at any time, but you cannot change the order. The ability on Daredevil’s hero card allows you to play the top card of the Sense deck as if it were in your hand by paying its cost. **Q. When Bloody Mary (B)/Typhoid Mary (B) ( 111) flips, does her “When Revealed” ability or toughness keyword resolve?** 

A. Yes, the ability or keyword on her new faceup side resolves. 

###### **THE GETAWAY** 

**Q. Can the “Forced Interrupt” on Out Front ( 129A) remove threat from the main scheme if there is a crisis icon in play or a minion with the patrol keyword engaged with me?** 

A. Yes. Because the “ **Forced Interrupt** ” on Out Front is an encounter card effect, it ignores crisis icons and the patrol keyword. 

###### **PROTECTION RACKET** 

# **<u>FREQUENTLY</u> ASKED QUESTIONS** 

###### **DAREDEVIL** 

**Q. Can players other than Daredevil trigger the abilities on Sense upgrades?** 

A. No, only the player who controls an upgrade can trigger its abilities. 

###### **ECHO** 

**Q. When can I use the ability on Photographic Reflexes ( 40) to play an event tucked under Echo?** 

A. While Photographic Reflexes is in your hand, it can be used any time that you would be able to play the tucked event if the tucked event had been in your hand. For example, if the tucked event has an “ **Action** ” ability, you can use Photographic Reflexes any time you could take an action during the player phase. If the event is a “ **Response** ” ability, you can use Photographic Reflexes to play that event any time its ability would trigger. 

**Q. What happens to the tucked event after I use Photographic Reflexes ( 40) to play it?** 

A. That event is placed in its owner’s discard pile. 

**Q. When Change of Venue ( 141) swaps a main scheme for a new one, what happens to cards attached to the previous main scheme?** 

A. All cards attached to the previous main scheme are discarded. 

###### **THE RAFT BREAKOUT** 

**Q. When Baron Zemo ( 145) leaves play, what happens to allies with threat on them?** 

A. They return to being allies under their owner’s control, but the threat tokens stay. If Baron Zemo enters play again, those allies become minions again (in addition to any ally that Baron Zemo places a threat on). 

###### **KINGPIN** 

**Q. Does Black Cat ( 169) retain the villainous keyword while she is an ally?** 

A. Yes. The rules for villainous have been revised to function for allies (and heroes) who have it. 

###### **DISCOUNT X (TRAIT)** 

**Q. If a card with discount has multiple traits listed and my identity has each of those traits, does the cost reduction apply multiple times?** 

A. No, the cost reduction is applied only once if your identity has any number of matching traits.
