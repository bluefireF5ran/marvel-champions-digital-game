## Page 1: Scenario Pack
The Shadowland Scenario Pack contains three scenarios set in the Hand's sinister fortress, Shadowland, that can be
played individually or together as a campaign; one special encounter set with a host of villains; and three modular sets
that represent the ninjas, traps, and dark magic at the Hand's disposal.

**Expansion Symbol.** The cards are identified by a pagoda-shaped symbol (four stacked tiers) before each card's
collector number.

## Pages 2 to 3: Featured Terms and Keywords
- **First Player Action:** Only the first player can trigger this action (any time they may take a standard action).
- **Assault:** When a character makes a basic thwart against a scheme with the assault keyword, that character uses its
  ATK instead of its THW for that thwart. If that character is an ally, it takes consequential damage listed under its
  ATK after the thwart.
- **Hinder X:** When a card with hinder X enters play, place X threat on that card.
- **Linked (Card Title):** A card with linked cannot be included in the encounter deck or a player's deck. Instead, it is
  set aside at the start of the game if any encounter card or card in a player's deck includes the card that brings the
  linked card into play (indicated in the parentheses following the keyword). A linked card does not count toward the
  minimum or maximum deck size.
- **Patrol:** While a minion with patrol is engaged with a player, that player cannot use cards they control to thwart
  the main scheme.
- **Permanent:** A card with permanent cannot be defeated, leave play, or have any part of its text box blanked, except
  by card abilities in the same set (hero set, scenario set, or modular set).
- **Piercing:** An attack with piercing discards any tough status cards from the target of the attack before dealing
  damage.
- **Ranged:** An attack with ranged ignores the retaliate keyword.
- **Setup:** A card with setup begins the game in play.
- **Stalwart:** A character with stalwart cannot be stunned or confused.
- **Steady:** A character with steady can have one additional stunned status card and one additional confused status
  card. That character is not stunned unless they have two stunned status cards, and is not confused unless they have two
  confused status cards. After that character's activation is replaced by a status card effect, remove all status cards of
  that type from that character.
- **Teamwork (TRAIT):** After a minion with teamwork enters play and engages a player, if there is at least one other
  minion that shares the specified trait in play, the minion that just entered play will activate against the player it
  is engaged with.
- **Villainous:** When a character with villainous activates, give it a facedown boost card from the top of the
  encounter deck. When you resolve that character's activation, turn the boost card faceup, resolve any boost ability on
  that card, and apply its boost icons to the character's stats for this activation. Discard the boost card after the
  activation.

## Page 4: Modular Sets, Special Encounter Set, Campaign Mode
- **Modular sets.** Three: **Black Flower, Deadly Contraptions, Ninja Swarm.** Each can be used in any scenario.
- **Special encounter set.** **Dark Protégé** has 16 cards in total: six villain cards and 10 other encounter cards
  linked to those villains. Its cards cannot be used outside the Shadowland Scenario Pack.
- **Campaign mode.** The three scenarios can be played individually or sequentially as a campaign. To complete the
  campaign the players must win all three scenarios in order. Each player uses their chosen identity for the entire
  campaign but may change aspects and alter the deck between scenarios (RRG deck customization).

## Page 5: Campaign Mode (continued)
- Set up each scenario as normal, then follow that scenario's Campaign Instructions in the order listed. When the game
  ends, if the players won follow the victory instructions in order; if they lost they may reset the scenario and try
  again with no penalty.
- **Prohibited cards.** Cards from the **Black Flower** modular set are required by some Campaign Instructions, so it
  cannot be included as a modular set in any scenario while playing the campaign.
- **Campaign log** (back cover, p. 20): records each scenario's results. During a campaign certain villains from the
  Dark Protégé set are checked off in the log; a checked-off villain is removed from the campaign and cannot be used in
  later scenarios.

## Page 6: Expert Campaign
Some Setup and Victory instructions are preceded by **Expert Campaign Only**; ignore them unless playing an expert
campaign.
- **Persistent Damage.** In the expert campaign each player records remaining hit points in the campaign log after they
  win a game; this is each player's starting hit points for the next scenario. If the remaining value is greater than
  the base hit points, record the base value instead.
- The setup of each scenario offers each player the chance to restore their identity to its starting hit point value by
  dealing themselves one facedown encounter card.
- If a player is defeated during a scenario and teammates go on to win, the defeated player does not take part in that
  scenario's victory steps, but can rejoin for the next scenario by dealing themselves one facedown encounter card during
  setup to restore their identity to its starting hit point value.

## Page 7: Scenario #1 Shadows in the Night (story)
A ninja clan, the Hand, builds Shadowland in Hell's Kitchen. A moonlit silhouette stands atop the tenshu, shouts
commands, and countless ninjas surround the fortress; the figure leaps to the streets below and attacks.

## Page 8: Scenario #1 New Rules
### Villain and Hero Positions
During setup, two double-sided environments are put into play (each named "The Streets Below" on one side and "Atop the
Tenshu" on the other). One of the environments represents the villain's position outside of Shadowland and has the
**VILLAIN POSITION** trait; the other represents the hero's position and has the **HERO POSITION** trait.

Some card abilities reference the faceup card with the VILLAIN POSITION trait, which is the villain's position. For
example, the text "If the villain's position is Atop the Tenshu..." indicates the environment with that trait must have
its Atop the Tenshu side faceup for that ability's condition to be met. If a card ability instructs players to "change
the villain's position," flip the card with the VILLAIN POSITION trait to its other side.

### Same and Different Positions
The Streets Below / Atop the Tenshu environment with the VILLAIN POSITION trait has two abilities: one says "If both
positions are the same..." while the other says "If the positions are different...". Both positions are the same if the
VILLAIN POSITION environment and the HERO POSITION environment share a faceup title, such as if each card's Atop the
Tenshu side is faceup. The positions are different if the two environments do not share a title.

## Page 9: Scenario #1 Campaign Instructions
**Setup**
- Each player records their identity in the campaign log. Players cannot switch identities during a campaign. If
  Daredevil's name or Elektra's name is recorded for this campaign, check off the villain with that name in the campaign
  log, then remove that villain from the campaign.
- Put 1 **Creeping Corruption** upgrade (collector 72A) from the Black Flower modular set into play under each player's
  control, Setup keyword side faceup. Set any remaining Creeping Corruption upgrades aside. Shuffle each other card from
  that set into the encounter deck.
- Place an additional 1 [per_hero] threat on the main scheme.

**Victory**
- In the campaign log, check off the name of the villain from the Dark Protégé special encounter set used in this
  scenario. Remove that villain from the campaign.
- Each player records if their Creeping Corruption upgrade has the **OVERPOWERING** trait in the campaign log.

## Page 10: Scenario #1 Victory (continued)
- Each player may choose one support or upgrade they control with a cost of 2 or less and record it in the campaign log.
  (Each player will be able to start the next scenario with their chosen card in play at the cost of additional
  corruption counters on their Creeping Corruption.) Players cannot choose cards without a cost (cards with a cost of
  "-").
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Record each identity's remaining hit points in the campaign log.

## Page 11: Scenario #2 Shadow Labyrinth (story)
The compound is a maze of shifting corridors and dead ends, with tripwires, poison darts and deadly contraptions. A
mysterious figure strikes from the shadows and fades from sight; the walls lurch in sudden shifts, leaving no time to
think.

## Page 12: Scenario #2 New Rules: The Trap Deck
The Deadly Contraptions modular set includes seven **TRAP!** treachery cards. During setup, in addition to creating the
encounter deck, find each TRAP! treachery from the encounter deck and shuffle them together with the Deadly Contraptions
set and the one TRAP! attachment, **Whisper in the Dark**, from the Shadow Labyrinth set. Together, this forms the 8-card
trap deck. Additionally, if players customize this scenario by using other modular sets that have cards with the TRAP!
trait, include each of those cards in the trap deck as well.

- When a player is instructed to reveal a card from the trap deck, that player reveals the top card of that deck and
  resolves the card as normal.
- Whenever a card with the TRAP! trait would be discarded, place that card into a special discard pile known as the trap
  discard pile (next to the trap deck).
- Once the trap deck is empty, shuffle the cards in the trap discard pile together to form a new trap deck. This is not
  considered resetting the encounter deck (including for the purpose of card abilities), and does not place an
  acceleration token on the main scheme.

## Page 13: Scenario #2 New Rules (continued)
- Certain card abilities may shuffle cards from the encounter deck into the trap deck. Whenever a card without the TRAP!
  trait is discarded, place that card in the encounter discard pile, even if it was previously drawn from the trap deck.
- Because cards from the encounter deck and the trap deck share the same card back, it is advised to keep the two decks
  (and their discard piles) in clearly separated sections of the game area.

### Infinite Hit Points
In this scenario the Shifting Corridors villain has infinite hit points (infinity symbol). A character with infinite hit
points cannot be defeated by taking damage. However, damage may still be dealt to that character through attacks and card
abilities.

### Passage Counters
During setup, a number of passage counters are placed on the Shifting Corridors villain. Various card abilities remove
passage counters from Shifting Corridors, and if no passage counters remain, the players lose the game. Because
Shifting Corridors has infinite hit points, its hit points do not need to be tracked; players are encouraged to use the
villain's hit point dial to track either the number of passage counters remaining on Shifting Corridors or the amount of
threat on the Into the Labyrinth main scheme.

## Page 14: Scenario #2 Campaign Instructions: Setup
- Put 1 Creeping Corruption upgrade (72A) from the Black Flower modular set into play under each player's control, Setup
  keyword side faceup. Set any remaining Creeping Corruption upgrades aside. Shuffle each other card from that set into
  the encounter deck.
- Each player checks the campaign log. Each player whose Creeping Corruption had the OVERPOWERING trait in the previous
  scenario places 1 corruption counter on their Creeping Corruption.
- Each player may take one copy of the card they recorded in the campaign log from their deck and put it into play under
  their control. Each player who does so places 1 corruption counter on their Creeping Corruption.
- Place an additional 3 [per_hero] threat on the main scheme.
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Set each player's hit points to their remaining hit point value recorded in the campaign log
  for the previous scenario.
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Each player may deal themselves one facedown encounter card to heal their identity to its
  full hit point value.

## Page 15: Scenario #2 Victory
- Each player records if their Creeping Corruption upgrade has the OVERPOWERING trait in the campaign log.
- Each player may choose one support or upgrade they control with a cost of 2 or less and record it in the campaign log
  (same wording as scenario #1; cards without a cost cannot be chosen).
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Record each identity's remaining hit points in the campaign log.

## Page 16: Scenario #3 Heart of Shadow (story)
In the final chamber the Hand's leader and master of evil magic, Krahllak, casts a ritual that will spread through New
York City like the roots of a black flower. He redirects it onto you; an overpowering sense of creeping corruption gnaws at
your resolve, threatening to turn you into one of Krahllak's dark protégés.

## Page 17: Scenario #3 Campaign Instructions
**Setup**
- Each player checks the campaign log and places 1 corruption counter on their Creeping Corruption upgrade for each time
  in a previous scenario their Creeping Corruption had the OVERPOWERING trait.
- Each player may take one copy of each card they recorded in the campaign log from their deck and put it into play under
  their control. Then, for each card a player put into play this way, that player places 1 corruption counter on their
  Creeping Corruption.
- Find the four minions from the Dark Protégé set (Archpriestess, Gorgon, Lady Bullseye, and White Tiger). Deal one
  minion at random to each player as a facedown encounter card, then shuffle any remaining minions into the encounter
  deck.
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Set each player's hit points to their remaining hit point value recorded in the campaign log
  for the previous scenario.
- **Expert Campaign Only:** Each player may deal themselves one facedown encounter card to heal their identity to its
  full hit point value.

**Victory**
- The players win the campaign!

## Page 18: Frequently Asked Questions
- **Q.** An effect on The Streets Below reads "Place 3 threat on the main scheme and stun your identity." Can I resolve
  this if my identity is already stunned? **A.** Yes, but be aware that the environment will not flip as a result. The
  ability that allows a player to choose the effect reads "Resolve one of the effects below, then flip this card." Due to
  the presence of "then," the chosen effect must be resolved in its entirety.
- **Q.** If I reveal a card with surge from the trap deck, where does the next revealed card come from? **A.** After you
  reveal a card with surge from the trap deck, the additional revealed encounter card comes from the encounter deck as
  normal.
- **Q.** If Krahllak attacks me, but one of my teammates defends against the attack, who resolves Krahllak's ability?
  **A.** Because Krahllak has an **Interrupt** ability that resolves "when Krahllak attacks you," you must choose whether
  or not to resolve Krahllak's ability before your teammate decides to defend.
- **Q.** When I defeat a NINJA minion and resolve its "When Defeated" ability, what happens if that ability puts a minion
  with the Teamwork (NINJA) keyword into play? **A.** Only after resolving the original minion's "When Defeated" ability
  and discarding it does the new minion's Teamwork ability check for another NINJA minion in play.
